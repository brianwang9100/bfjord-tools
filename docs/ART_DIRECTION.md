# Art direction and visual backlog

BFjord Tools currently demonstrates working, asset-backed Unity Editor authoring across five core families: terrain, roads, bridges, foliage and connected water. The curated captures are evidence that these systems compose and render in the bounded fixtures. They are not a claim of finished production art or NatureManufacture visual parity.

## Current visual character

The intended setting is a restrained temperate coast: readable landforms, cool connected water, dark road ribbons, grounded bridge materials and clustered vegetation that follows slopes, clearances and bank fields. Materials should remain legible under ordinary daylight without hiding geometry behind dramatic exposure or post-processing.

The current bridge collection has the clearest authored silhouettes and material identities. The landscape tools produce deterministic, editable Unity assets and the ten-batch habitat composition gives the site useful canopy, understory, ground and riverbank layers. Terrain, shoreline and roadside staging remain simpler and more generic than the bridge work.

The legacy gray structure/tunnel group is a lifecycle utility fixture. It was removed from the curated landscape, and no tunnel beauty target is claimed for this release.

## Candid limitations

- Terrain macro-forms are sparse in some views, with broad slopes that still read as a staging surface rather than a fully authored coastal place.
- Ground materials need stronger scale cues and more controlled transitions between grass, exposed earth, rock, verge and wet bank.
- Shoreline lighting and shallow-water contact can look uniform. Depth color, wetness and restrained foam need more local variation while preserving the finite connected-water contract.
- Road edges are mechanically clean. Shoulder width, aggregate breakup, verge encroachment and occasional wear need authored variation without changing the accepted road geometry or clearance receipts.
- Foliage placement is materially richer after the ten-batch pass, but species repetition, canopy rhythm and empty-to-dense transitions still need composition review from road level and elevated views.
- Sky, sun, reflection and exposure need one consistent gallery setup so material comparisons remain honest across captures.
- LOD transitions and wind have bounded implementation contracts, but their moving-camera appearance and device cost remain unverified.

## Next visual pass

1. Lock a neutral daylight, exposure and reflection setup for the landscape cameras.
2. Refine terrain staging around the road, lake and river so the scene has deliberate foreground, middle distance and skyline shapes.
3. Improve road shoulders and verge variation while preserving the 29.872583% pavement-grade ceiling, Terrain clearance and ownership receipts.
4. Add localized shoreline depth, wet-bank and foam variation at lake, river and ocean contacts without implying fluid simulation.
5. Tune the ten habitat batches as one composition: protect sightlines, vary canopy rhythm, strengthen bank planting and remove obvious repeated silhouettes.
6. Review material scale and response on all four bridges under the same light, keeping the proven geometry-independent surface workflow.
7. Inspect representative LOD and wind transitions in motion before making any runtime or mobile-quality claim.

Visual review should use paired road-level and elevated views with stable camera, light and quality settings. Repairs should be judged by contact, scale, silhouette and material response rather than triangle count or screenshot saturation. The authoring receipts remain authoritative for geometry, grade, ownership and restoration; screenshots remain art evidence only.
